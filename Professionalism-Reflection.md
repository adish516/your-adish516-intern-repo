🔍 Research & Learn


What are examples of professional vs. unprofessional behaviour in a casual work environment? (Hint: Think about communication, meeting etiquette, and teamwork.)
I think a casual work environment means you can be yourself and have friendly conversations, but you still need to take your work seriously. Being on time, listening in meetings, doing what you agreed to do and letting people know if you’re running behind are all part of that.

Unprofessional behaviour would be interrupting people, ignoring messages, making inappropriate jokes or missing deadlines without saying anything. Being friendly with someone doesn’t mean their time matters any less.

What does respectful communication look like in remote teams? (e.g., tone in messages, handling disagreements, responding to feedback)
Messages can come across differently from how you intended, so I would try to be clear and considerate. I wouldn’t assume someone was being rude just because their reply was short or they took a while to respond.

If I disagree with something, I can explain why without making it personal. I would also respect people’s working hours and give everyone space to share their ideas.

How can you give and receive feedback professionally? (e.g., focus on actions, not people; assume good intent; ask for clarification instead of reacting defensively)
When giving feedback, I would focus on the work and explain what could be improved. For example, instead of saying an outreach message is bad, I could suggest making the purpose clearer or adding a next step.

When receiving feedback, I would try to understand what the person means before responding. If something is unclear, I would ask for an example. I might have a reason for doing something a certain way, but I should still be open to changing it.

📝 Reflection

Have you ever experienced or witnessed great teamwork and professionalism? What made it effective?
I was in a group for my university group and they were really mature and did all the communication were professionally and effectively and ended up getting a high grade.

For me, good teamwork means knowing what everyone is responsible for and feeling comfortable asking for help. It’s easier to work together when people communicate early instead of leaving problems until the last minute.

What steps can you take to make sure your communication is clear, respectful, and inclusive?
I would give enough context so the other person knows what I’m talking about, explain what I need and mention any deadline. I would avoid
unnecessary jargon and check that we have the same understanding.

I also want to be mindful that people have different backgrounds and ways of communicating. I wouldn’t assume everyone is comfortable with the same jokes or communication style.

How can you help create a positive, fun, and professional work environment?
I can contribute by being approachable, showing interest in other people’s ideas and recognising their effort. I think there should be room to have fun and get to know each other while still getting the work done.

I would offer help when I can, but also be honest about my capacity rather than agree to something I can’t finish.

How can you prepare effectively for meetings with supervisors and colleagues?
Before a meeting, I would check what it’s about, review my tasks and write down any questions. I would have a short update ready about what I’ve finished, what I’m working on and anything I’m stuck on.

During the meeting, I would take notes and make sure I understand what I need to do next. Afterwards, I would update my tasks so I don’t forget what we agreed on.

What would it look like to be proactive during your internship?
For my partnership marketing role, being proactive could mean researching potential partners and suggesting why they might be a good fit for Focus Bear. I can also bring ideas for outreach instead of waiting for someoneo tell me every step.

If I get stuck, I would check the instructions and try to work through the problem first. If I still need help, I would explain what I’ve tried. I would also check with my supervisor before contacting external partners
or making commitments for the company.

How might you follow up with colleagues or supervisors if you need information/action from them? How would you escalate it further if you don't get a response and when would you escalate?
If I need something from someone, I would explain what I need and when I need it. I would follow the team’s expectations for response times. For something non-urgent, I would generally follow up after one or two working days, keeping their schedule in mind.

What are some things you won't do?
1.Say I’ve completed something when I haven’t.
2.Copy AI-generated work without checking it or understanding it.
3.Share private company information in a public repository.
4.Ignore feedback or make disagreements personal.
5.Keep messaging someone repeatedly about a non-urgent task.
6.Hide a mistake or leave it until the deadline to ask for help.
