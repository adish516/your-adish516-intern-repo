# Using AI Tools Responsibly

## AI tools relevant to my role

For my partnership marketing role, tools like ChatGPT could help with
brainstorming, organising research and drafting outreach messages.
Writing assistants could also help check grammar and make a message
clearer.

I would check which tools Focus Bear allows before using them for
anything involving company information.

## Benefits and risks

I think AI is useful when I have an idea but don’t know how to structure
it, or when I need a starting point for a task. It can suggest different
approaches and help me spot things I might have missed.

The risk is that an answer can sound convincing even when it’s wrong.
It can also produce generic messages that don’t really suit properly. If I copy something without understanding it, I’m still
responsible for what I submit or send and that can be very unprofessional.

## Information I shouldn’t enter into AI tools

I wouldn’t enter passwords, access tokens, verification codes or other
login details.

I also wouldn’t put private user information, confidential partner
conversations, contracts or unpublished company plans into an unapproved
AI tool. Having access to information doesn’t automatically mean I have
permission to share it with another service.

If I’m unsure, I would ask first or use a made-up example that doesn’t
include identifying details.

## Checking AI-generated content

I would check factual claims against reliable sources, especially
official product pages when researching other apps. I would open the
sources myself rather than assume a link supports what the AI says.

For outreach, I would check the organisation’s name, what it does and
whether the suggested partnership actually makes sense. I would also
remove claims or promises that I can’t support.

## When I would use AI and when I would use my own judgement

I would use AI to help organise ideas, suggest an outline or improve
the wording of a draft.

My own experiences, opinions and observations need to come from me.
For example, AI can help explain an issue I noticed in the app, but
it shouldn’t invent something I experienced.

Deciding whether a potential partner is suitable also needs my judgement
and the team’s input. I wouldn’t treat an AI recommendation as approval
to contact someone. 

Also sometimes for grammar, AI can be helpful.

## Avoiding over-reliance

I want to write down my own thoughts before asking AI for help, even
if they’re just rough points. That way, I’m giving it something to
work with rather than letting it decide everything for me.

Before using the output, I should be able to explain it in my own
words. If I don’t understand a claim or recommendation, I need to
look into it instead of just keeping it because it sounds professional.

## Keeping information private

I would use approved tools and only share the minimum information
needed for the task. Before pasting anything, I would check for names,
contact details, account information and confidential content.

Removing a name might not be enough if the remaining details still
identify someone. Where possible, I would use public information or
a fictional example instead.

## A task I tried with AI

I used ChatGPT to help draft my competitive landscape reflection
comparing Focus Bear with Freedom, Cold Turkey, Tiimo and Routinery.

It helped organise the comparison and turn the information into a
clear draft. I also asked for more natural wording because some of
the earlier phrasing didn’t sound like me.

One point that needed checking was whether Focus Bear has an equivalent
to Tiimo’s AI co-planner. The draft said an equivalent wasn’t found in
Focus Bear’s public feature information. That isn’t the same as proving
the feature doesn’t exist, so I would need to check with the team before
making a definite claim.

This showed me that AI can help with structure and wording, but the
research and conclusions still need reviewing. I also shouldn’t describe
a website comparison as hands-on testing of all the apps.

## One best practice I will follow

Before submitting anything I’ve used AI to help with, I’ll check:
is it accurate, does it reflect what I actually think or did, and can
I explain it myself?

If the answer is no, it needs more work before I use it.
