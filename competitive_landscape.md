# Competitive Landscape

## Comparing the apps

| App | What stood out to me |
|---|---|
| Freedom | Focuses on blocking distracting apps and websites across devices. It also offers scheduled sessions and a Locked Mode. |
| Cold Turkey | Focuses on strict desktop blocking, with features that make locked blocks difficult to bypass. |
| Tiimo | Focuses on visual planning, with a daily timeline and AI that breaks tasks down and helps schedule them. |
| Routinery | Focuses on building routines through timed activities and following habits in a set order. |
| Focus Bear | Combines distraction blocking, guided routines and break reminders in one app. |

## What makes Focus Bear different?

I think the main difference is how Focus Bear combines several types
of support. Freedom and Cold Turkey put more emphasis on blocking
distractions, while Tiimo focuses on planning and Routinery focuses
on following routines.

Focus Bear brings blocking, routines and breaks together. That could
be useful for someone who needs help with more than just staying
off distracting websites.

I wouldn’t say every feature is unique, though. Freedom also works
across devices, and Routinery also guides people through routines.
The combination is what stands out to me.

## Why would I choose Focus Bear?

I would choose Focus Bear if I wanted help with both staying focused
and creating more structure in my day. Having routines, blocking and
break reminders together could be more convenient than using
separate apps for each.

For balancing uni, internship work and personal projects, that
combination sounds useful to me.

But I don’t think it would automatically be the best option for
everyone. If I only wanted distraction blocking, I would also
consider Freedom or Cold Turkey. If planning my day was the main
problem, Tiimo might be a better fit. If I mainly wanted a timer
to guide me through habits, I would consider Routinery.

## What is one feature a competitor has that Focus Bear doesn't?

Tiimo has an AI co-planner that can turn typed or spoken thoughts
into smaller tasks, estimate how long they might take and organise
them into a schedule.

I couldn’t find an equivalent feature in Focus Bear’s public feature
information. I would confirm this with the team before saying it
definitely doesn’t exist.

This stood out because sometimes the difficulty is figuring out
where to start, even before distractions become an issue.

## What is one improvement Focus Bear could make?

I think a useful improvement would be a simple way to break a large
task into smaller steps before starting a focus session.

For example, if I entered “research potential partners,” it could
help me turn that into steps like choosing a partner category,
finding five organisations and writing down why each might fit.

That would connect planning with actually doing the work. I would
keep it optional so it doesn’t add more setup for someone who
already knows what they want to focus on.
