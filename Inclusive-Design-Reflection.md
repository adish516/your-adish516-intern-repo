# Inclusive Design Reflection

## Understanding different needs

I think it’s important to remember that people don’t all experience
the same app in the same way. Something that feels obvious to me
might be confusing or overwhelming for someone else.

People can face extra barriers because of disability, age, language,
limited digital experience or their circumstances. Neurodivergent
people aren’t automatically vulnerable or unable to manage things,
but an inaccessible design can put them at a disadvantage.

In digital spaces, barriers could include unclear instructions,
too many notifications, distracting visuals or difficulty finding
help when something goes wrong.

## Being respectful and ethical

I wouldn’t assume that everyone with ADHD or autism has the same
needs. I would ask what works for someone rather than decide for them.

Users should understand what the app does, what information it collects
and what choices they have. Marketing should also be honest about
what the product can offer, without making people feel bad about
their difficulties or promising that an app will fix everything.

I think involving users in decisions is important too. We shouldn’t
assume something is helpful without listening to the people using it.

## Making communication more accessible

I would use clear language, short sections and specific next steps.
If there are several things to do, I would put them in order instead
of writing one long paragraph.

I would avoid saying something is “easy” or “obvious,” because that
can make someone feel worse if they’re struggling with it.

For teammates, I would be clear about priorities and deadlines,
provide written instructions and allow time to process information.
I wouldn’t interpret a short reply or a different communication
style as someone being rude or uninterested.

## Things that could make an app less supportive

Too many setup steps at once could make it harder to get started.
Unexplained blocking, sudden notifications or unclear buttons could
also leave someone unsure about what’s happening.

Messages that make people feel guilty for missing a routine would
be another issue. I think it’s more useful to help someone find
their next step than make them feel like they’ve failed.

Predictable navigation and options to adjust reminders, sounds
and activities could help people use the app in a way that suits them.

## First-person account

Jeremy Nagel’s founder’s note describes how he wanted to follow
a morning routine but would end up checking emails and losing time.
He explains that the tools he tried didn’t consistently help,
which led him to create Focus Bear.

My takeaway is that wanting to do something doesn’t always mean
the tools or structure are there to make it manageable. A productivity
tool needs to fit the person using it.

This is one person’s experience, though, and I wouldn’t assume
it represents everyone with ADHD or autism.

## One improvement I would suggest

When I first used Focus Bear, some apps wouldn’t open and I didn’t
realise it was waiting for me to explain why I needed them.

I would suggest making that prompt more noticeable and explaining
what’s happening before asking the user to respond. For example:

“Focus Bear has paused access to this app during your focus session.
If you need it for your current task, explain why in the box below.”

The exact wording should match how the feature works. I think this
would reduce the confusion I experienced and make the next step clearer.

## A practical change in my own work

For partnership messages and other content, I’ll make the main point
and next step easy to find. I’ll avoid sending several different
requests in one long message.

I also want to check whether my wording is supportive rather than
making assumptions about someone’s abilities or needs.

## Practice response to a user

Hypothetical message:
“I keep missing my routine and now there are too many things to do.
I don’t know where to start.”

My response:
“Hey, that sounds overwhelming and so valid. You don’t need to catch up on everything
at once. Would it help to choose just one activity to start with today?

If the app itself is making that difficult, let me know which screen
you’re on. We can work through one step at a time.”
